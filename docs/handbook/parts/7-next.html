5 · What is leftNot built, not checked, and what comes next

Nine days before the demo: what a reader might assume exists, and does not.

Not built

PieceWhat there is today instead
A map in the app; the agent's position; a time of arrival An area from a list or a GPS fix. Trip distances are straight lines times 1.7. A maps provider is chosen and a key for its tiles exists; no map is in the app.
A bag that comes back to the hub The order ends as "delivery failed" with a reason. Shelf or write-off is not built.
Releasing a locked code; scanning a label Five wrong codes end a hand-over as not delivered. A bag is confirmed by a tap.
The hub's dispatch panel as a page Bags waiting, trips and agents on duty are in the API. No page shows them.
A simulated day of many orders Pooling is a claim about many orders. Every number in section 4 comes from one order, a scripted batch of orders, or a load test.
Hubs asking farms for produce The farmer offers; the hub does not ask. The forecast it would ask from exists.
Real payment, refunds, paying farmers Payment is a stand-in. A farmer's accepted weight and asking price are only recorded.
A production environment; a second copy of the app; real staff accounts Staging only, one copy, shared demo logins for hub staff. Each is a decision not to do it before the demo.

Built, but not checked

  • An order from the installed app. So far it has only been installed and started, on one phone.
  • Several bags on one trip, end to end. Tests cover it. The end-to-end run places one order.
  • Load on the new code. The load test is from 23 November and calls neither orders nor delivery.
  • The deploy script's newest step was tried by hand and has not run inside a deploy.

Known limits of what is built

  • Settings such as 4 orders, 10 minutes, 6 per trip and 30% are starting values, not measured ones.
  • The bill follows the pack, not the actual weight.
  • Bangalore's two hubs still carry the packaged groceries of the dark-store prototype this project grew from. About six hundred tests are written against that data.
  • Eleven of the app's 40 photographs need credit, which the app does not show yet.
