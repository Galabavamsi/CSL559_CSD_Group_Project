4 · The micro-warehouseHow a hub works

A hub does three things: it lets good produce in, it knows exactly what is on which shelf, and it never promises a pack it does not have. Each rule below is in the code and has a test.

Letting produce in

  • Which hub. The nearest open hubs within 60 km of the farm are tried nearest first. A hub is passed over if it has no shelves of the item's temperature zone, or no room for the weight.
  • Room counts what is coming. Free room = capacity − produce on the shelves − lots already on their way. Without the last term, ten farmers listing in one hour would all be sent to the same nearly full hub.
  • When. The next morning intake window, 06:00 to 09:00 local time, never before the harvest date.
  • Weigh and grade once. Received kg, rejected kg, a grade and a remark. The farmer is judged on rejected ÷ delivered over 30 days, shown beside each expected drop.
  • Kilograms into units. Weights are whole grams. Accepted grams ÷ pack size = whole packs; what does not fill a pack is recorded as trim. The lot becomes batch L<listing> with expiry = harvest date + shelf life.
  • Too old is refused. A batch with less than half its shelf life left is recorded and never stocked.
  • A lot is taken in once. Two receivers scanning the same lot at the same moment take turns; the second is told it is done.

Only one module writes stock. Every test that moves stock ends by checking these two lines.

  • Counts. A picker who finds a shelf empty corrects the count on the spot, and a count of that bin is opened. Small differences are approved automatically (under ₹500); larger ones wait for a manager.
  • Dates. Expired batches are written off every hour. A batch within 2 days of its date raises an event.
  • Who did it. Every staff call is in an audit log, refusals included.

Never promising what is not there

The shop shows available = on hand − held. Checkout does not remove stock; it holds it, with one statement per line of the cart that carries its own check:

UPDATE stock_levels
   SET qty_reserved = qty_reserved + :qty
 WHERE store_id = :hub AND sku_id = :item
   AND qty_on_hand - qty_reserved >= :qty
-- 0 rows changed: not enough stock

The database runs these one at a time for a given row, so two carts cannot both take the last pack. A test has twenty buyers race for one unit: one wins.

  • Whole cart or nothing. If any line cannot be held, none is.
  • Ten minutes. An unpaid hold expires and a sweep every 30 seconds gives the stock back.
  • Paying creates the pick job. Bins are chosen then, oldest batch first (first expiry, first out).
  • One picker per order. Claiming a job is the same kind of guarded update, so two pickers cannot take one order.
  • Busy hours. With 4 or more orders waiting, a picker takes 3 in one walk, one tote each.
  • Empty shelf. The line moves to another bin, or a substitute from the same kind of produce that is never dearer is offered, or the line is dropped and refunded.
  • Packing commits the sale. Until then a cancel only releases a hold; no count is ever "put back".
  • Stuck orders. No scan for 5 minutes and the order returns to the queue.

Planning ahead

QuestionHow the hub answers it
How much will sell tomorrow?A daily forecast per item. Three simple models are compared by measured error (WAPE) and the best is used; reorder points follow from it.
Which shelf for which item?Slotting: each zone's items are ranked by 14 days of sales and the fast sellers get the bins nearest the packing desk.
Where should the next hub go?Every address lookup is saved by H3 cell, served or turned away. The network map picks the sites that would cover the most turned-away customers.
What needs attention now?The ops dashboard lists stalled orders, bags waiting and items out of stock first, then every KPI beside its target.
