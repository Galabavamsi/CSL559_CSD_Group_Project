3 · One lot, start to finishForty kilograms of tomatoes

Every picture on these pages is the real backend: the phone app and the hub's pages driving one PostgreSQL database, recorded on 23 November 2026. The video shows the same run.

1

The farmer lists a lot

Ravi signs in with a code sent to his email, registers his farm near Bhilai where he stands, and lists what he picked this morning. The backend looks for the nearest hub that has shelves of the right temperature and room for the weight, and gives him a slot.

2

The hub weighs it, grades it and shelves it

At the dock the receiver's phone lists the lots on their way. She records what arrived and what is turned away, and gives a grade. The accepted weight is packed into the item's smallest unit, and the phone names the bin for each portion.

3

The customer orders

Asha signs in the same way and says where she lives. The hub whose zone covers the address serves her, and the shop lists what that hub can deliver now. Holding the cart sets two packs aside at the hub; paying confirms the order.

4

The hub picks and packs

A picker's phone hands out the next paid order, one line at a time: which bin, which batch, how many. Every unit is scanned. Stock on hand goes down only when the order is packed.

5

Both phones see the result

The farmer sees the hub's verdict on his lot. The customer sees her order reach Packed, which is as far as the backend goes today.
